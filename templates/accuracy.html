How accurate is it?

Measured, not estimated. Every number here comes from reports/metrics.json, which the training script writes. The short answer: very accurate on documents like the ones it learned from, noticeably less on documents from elsewhere.

Why the first number is too optimistic. Each document type was learned mostly from a single public dataset. A model can partly learn "which dataset is this from" instead of "what kind of document is this", and the held-out test shares those datasets. The second number, from new sources, is closer to what you should expect.

A mistake that changed the training data

{% set l = m.get('leave_one_source_out', {}) %}

The first version learned research papers only from computer-science papers. It then called {{ l.get('pubmed', {}).get('predicted', {}).get('medical_report', 0) }} of {{ l.get('pubmed', {}).get('n', 0) }} medical research abstracts "medical reports": it had learned the topic, not the kind of document. Medical abstracts were added to the training data. Repeating the test the other way round (trained without computer-science papers) still gets only {{ '%.0f'|format(l.get('scitldr', {}).get('accuracy', 0) * 100) }}% of them right, so a research paper from a field it hasn't seen may still be misread.

Personal numbers

There's no labelled dataset of Indian personal numbers to test against, so the finder was run over all {{ ss.get('documents') }} training documents (from US, UK and Malaysian sources) and every hit was read by hand. It flagged {% for k, n in ss.get('hits', {}).items() %}{{ n }} {{ {'email': 'email addresses', 'phone': 'phone numbers', 'card': 'card numbers', 'account': 'bank account numbers', 'aadhaar': 'Aadhaar numbers', 'pan': 'PANs'}.get(k, k) }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %}, and no Aadhaar numbers or PANs. On review, the card numbers were real payment and loyalty cards printed on receipts and the phone numbers were real.

The first version also flagged product barcodes as card numbers and a timestamp as an Aadhaar number. The rules were tightened after that review, so this is a false-alarm check, not a held-out test.

Limits worth knowing

  • It only knows these 8 types. Anything else, like a party invitation, still gets one of the 8 labels, often with high confidence.
  • It reads English text only.
  • Photos and scans depend on OCR quality: blurred or skewed images give worse results.
  • The personal-number finder checks Aadhaar and card numbers with their check digits, but can still miss numbers written in unusual ways or flag a look-alike.
